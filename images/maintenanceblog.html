The Essentials of Routine Auto Maintenance

Staying on top of routine automotive maintenance is the easiest way to protect your engine, keep your fuel economy strong, and avoid unexpected breakdowns. Small, affordable services done on schedule prevent the kind of major wear that leads to expensive repairs down the road.

Key Maintenance Services Every Driver Needs

Whether you commute up and down I-15 or drive around town, these core maintenance items keep your vehicle running smoothly:

  • Oil & Filter Changes: Fresh engine oil lubricates moving parts, reduces heat, and keeps sludge from building up inside your engine. Changing your oil and filter on schedule—typically every 3,000 to 7,500 miles depending on your oil type—is the single best thing you can do to extend engine life.
  • Spark Plugs & Tune-Ups: Spark plugs ignite the air-fuel mixture in your engine thousands of times per minute. Worn plugs cause rough idling, sluggish acceleration, misfires, and poor gas mileage.
  • Engine Air & Cabin Filters: A clogged engine air filter chokes your engine's performance, while a dirty cabin air filter reduces A/C airflow and lets dust and pollen into your interior.
  • Fluids & Belts: Checking your coolant, brake fluid, transmission fluid, power steering fluid, and serpentine belts regularly helps catch leaks and cracking before a hose or belt fails on the highway.
  • Battery & Charging Checks: Extreme Utah summers and freezing winters are tough on car batteries. Testing your battery and alternator ahead of seasonal shifts helps you avoid getting stranded with a no-start.

Signs Your Vehicle Is Overdue for Maintenance

Even if you haven't hit your mileage mark yet, your car will often let you know when it's time for a checkup:

  • Check Engine or Maintenance Light: Never ignore a dashboard warning—a quick diagnostic scan can pinpoint the issue early.
  • Drop in Fuel Economy: Dirty filters, old spark plugs, or overdue oil changes make your engine work harder and burn more gas.
  • Rough Idling or Hard Starts: Shaking at stoplights or slow cranking in the morning often points to worn spark plugs or a weak battery.
  • Unusual Squealing or Burning Smells: Squealing under the hood can mean a worn belt, while a burning smell may indicate a fluid leak onto hot engine components.
