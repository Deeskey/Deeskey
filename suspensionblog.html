Your Complete Guide to Steering & Suspension

Your suspension system does a lot more than just give you a smooth ride—it keeps your tires planted firmly on the road, stabilizes your handling, and helps you stay in control around every corner and sudden stop. Because suspension parts wear down gradually, it’s easy to get used to a rough ride without realizing your components are overdue for service.

Key Parts of Your Suspension System

Every bump, pothole, and turn is absorbed by a network of heavy-duty parts working together beneath your vehicle:

  • Shocks & Struts: Shocks and struts control the bounce of your springs so your car doesn't keep bouncing after hitting a bump. Struts are also a structural part of your suspension and support the weight of the vehicle alongside the coil spring.
  • Control Arms & Ball Joints: Upper and lower control arms connect your wheel hubs to the vehicle's frame, allowing the wheels to move up and down smoothly while keeping them aligned. Worn bushings or loose ball joints can cause clunking and sloppy handling.
  • Tie Rods: Tie rods connect your steering rack to your front wheels. When tie rod ends wear out, your steering can feel loose, wander on the highway, or chew through your tires.
  • Sway Bars & End Links: These reduce body roll when you take corners, keeping your vehicle flat and predictable.

5 Warning Signs Your Suspension Needs Attention

Catching worn suspension parts early protects your tires and keeps your vehicle safe to drive. Watch out for these common signs:

  • Clunking, Knocking, or Squeaking Over Bumps: Noises when going over speed bumps or turning often point to worn control arm bushings, ball joints, or sway bar links.
  • Excessive Bouncing or Nose-Diving: If your car keeps bouncing after a dip in the road or dips forward heavily when you brake, your shocks or struts are likely worn out.
  • Pulling or Drifting to One Side: A vehicle that fights you to stay straight can have worn tie rods, a bad control arm, or alignment issues.
  • Uneven Tire Wear: Cupping, scalloping, or heavy wear on the inside or outside edges of your tires is a telltale sign of worn suspension components.
  • Leaking Fluid on Shocks or Struts: If you see oily residue coating the outside of a shock or strut, the internal seal has failed and it’s time for a replacement.

Why Replacing Worn Parts Matters

Ignoring a bad strut or worn upper control arm doesn't just make your commute uncomfortable—it puts extra stress on your remaining steering parts, increases your stopping distance, and can ruin a brand-new set of tires in a matter of months. Replacing worn components restores tight handling and keeps your vehicle safe in all Utah weather conditions.
